They always say
the devil you know
is better than the one you do not.

But no one ever asks
why he knows your name,
why he sounds like you
when the room becomes quiet.

Maybe he does not arrive
with horns,
fire,
or an offer written in blood.

Maybe he wakes when you do,
brushes the same teeth,
wears your favourite shirt,
and explains, quite reasonably,
why today is not the day to change.

Maybe the devil you know
knows every wound
because he was there when you made it.

He knows which memories to touch,
which fears to call wisdom,
which cages to rename comfort.

And perhaps that is why
you have never managed to escape him.

You have been searching
behind doors,
inside people,
beneath every misfortune—

when all this time
the devil you know
might be you.
